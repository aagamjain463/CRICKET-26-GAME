## The two root causes

1. **Single-period idle + non-monotone trigger.** The striker used the same `C26Motion::Rest()` single-sine-per-channel idle as every fielder, so he looped visibly; and the trigger ran through `sin(Load*PI)`, which peaks at half load and returns to zero on the delivery stride — the batter loaded his backlift while the bowler ran in and put the bat back down on the ball. Fixed with `C26Motion::BatterIdle` (four incommensurate periods + a gated bat tap) and `C26Motion::BatterTrigger` (`Press` monotone in load; separate `Step`/`Lift` curves so the relocating foot takes a step instead of scraping).

2. **A silent IK clamp that no gate could see.** `two_bone()` clamps an over-long target and returns a `reachable` flag that the authoring pass threw away. The front leg was asked for up to 9.0 cm more than it has, so the foot never reached the ground — the front ankle sat 7.1 cm high at contact and the corrector preserved that as a whole-body lift. **Fixed by driving the hips FORWARD over the front foot (`hips_y` 0.00 → 0.27), not by lowering them**; lowering raises the hip→foot span and makes it worse. Front knee went from locked-straight (100.5% extension) to 162→126→162°.

## Reusable lesson (the important one)

`worst_skate()`-style checks compare only frames a foot is **already** down on, so a foot that never arrives at the ground is invisible to them. Any "this limb is planted/attached" gate needs a companion gate that measures the limb's **distance from its own target**, not just frame-to-frame stability.

Also: when a limb is short of its target, check the CHAIN LENGTH first. The clamp ceiling is `(L1+L2)*0.999` — at f23 the realized ankle sat exactly on the hip→target ray at 76.28 cm while the chain is 76.36 cm, which identified a clamp rather than a solve bug.

## The imported clip is verified — and how
`Tools/VerifyImportedClip.py` (added this session) loads the imported `UAnimSequence` and re-asserts the drive's geometry in engine cm. **10/10 PASS**, agreeing with the offline corrector to 0.1 cm: stance ankles 24.7/24.7, hips 184.5, hand span 17.9, planted-foot worst lift 1.1 cm, weight 37.2 cm forward, front-foot stride 35.8 cm, contact hands 49.0 cm in front of the hips, head 107.1 cm above the hips, and **chest 36.5 deg off square** — which matches the corrector's own `chest closed +36.5 deg off square` exactly. The import is faithful.

**Reference-direction trap, and the near-miss it caused.** For a batsman the back-foot-to-front-foot line is NOT "down the pitch" — his feet are largely side by side across the crease, so that line is mostly LATERAL. Using it as the forward reference made the shoulder line look anti-parallel (dot −0.998) and I briefly suspected a whole-body 180° twist. It is not one: the chest NORMAL is 36.5° off the direction of travel, which is exactly a textbook slightly-closed stance, and the shoulder line is 90 + 36.5 = 126.5° from it, which is what was measured. **Use the direction the body TRAVELS (hips f0 → contact) as the bowler reference; it cannot be fooled.** Both checks are now permanent gates, and the chest one doubles as the 180°-yaw guard the pipeline has needed since `repair_facing` (a twist sends the chest normal to ~143° and fails).

**Composition gotcha, worth remembering:** with `u.Transform()` accumulation in the Python API, iterating `find_bone_path_to_root()` in its **natural order (bone first, root last)** is what reproduces the offline numbers; reversing it to root-first does not. That is a fact about *Python's* multiplication semantics and is independent of the gate bug above — do not "fix" the order. (Note the C++ convention is the opposite: `Acc = Acc * Local(child)` walking root-first is correct there, which is what the gate already does.)

## Round 3 fielding — finished the approach half of the premium fielding sequence

Continued from `13e6e81` ("ROUND 3: Premium reference fielding sequence"), which had committed the pickup → throw → follow-through solvers plus a Blender keyframe pass. A previous agent had left **uncommitted** refinements (a deceleration cross-fade in `C26Athlete.cpp`, a 120 Hz continuity test, and a new `Tools/FieldLab.sh`). Committed the finished work as **`5af1da0`**.

### The defect that mattered — and the trap behind it

The uncommitted deceleration code advanced the braking stride with `ApproachGait += Dt*ResidualCadence`. **On the real match path `Dt` is always zero**, so it never advanced.

The reason is worth remembering: `C26MatchGameMode` drives the whole fielding sequence by **assigning `ActionTime` and calling `Animate(0)`** — the `EventPose` test at `C26MatchGameMode.cpp:1745` (`I==ActiveFielder && (ThrowClock>=0||CatchClock>=0)`) forces the zero. The pose clock is authoritative; `Dt` is a no-op for these actions.

**Consequence / rule:** anything in a `Busy` action that is *integrated per frame* is dead code. Solve it in **closed form from `ActionTime`** instead. That also makes it frame-rate independent, which per-frame accumulation is not.

### What the fix is

- `SetAction()` captures `ApproachSpeed = max(ShownSpeed, MoveSpeed)` and `ApproachGait = GaitPhase` **only on the frame the action changes to `Pickup`** — the match squares the fielder up and zeroes `MoveSpeed` immediately afterwards, and the next `Animate(0)` snaps `ShownSpeed` to zero too. `ShownSpeed`/`GaitPhase` at that instant are exactly what the previous frame's `Running` pose used, so the hand-off is continuous.
- New pure solver `C26Motion::SolveApproachBrake(ActionTime, ApproachSpeed, ApproachGait, AnkleZ, ClipLength)` → `FApproachBrake{LeftFoot, RightFoot, PitchL, PitchR, Plant, ResidualSpeed, LeanForward, Crouch}`. Speed decays as `exp(-11t)`, gait integrated in closed form, gather cross-fades in over the first 0.16 s.
- `FApproachBrake::Plant` **defaults to 1** and the `ApproachSpeed <= 40` early-out returns the default. This is load-bearing: with `Plant == 0` the blend `Lerp(Brake.LeftFoot, FL, 0)` would teleport both feet to `(0,0,0)` — a far worse bug than the one being fixed.

### Testing trap I walked into — bounds must be speed-scaled

My first test asserted `foot travel < 4 cm per 1/120 s` (copied from the standing-solver bounds) and "decelerates step over step". **Both failed, and both were wrong, not the code:**

- A **support foot at 700 cm/s covers 5.8 cm in 1/120 s** — that is the ground going past, not a skate. A flat few-cm bound is a *standing-pose* bound; it fails every real run.
- Each foot's travel depends on where in the cycle it is; a swing leg snaps through faster than the body, so "step N+1 shorter than step N" is **not** an invariant.

Correct framing: bound the **planted** foot tightly (at least one foot is always in support in a stride, so `min(leftTravel, rightTravel)` is a planted foot), scale the pop bound by the carried speed, and assert the **residual speed** decays monotonically — that is the quantity that actually decays.

## Round 3 fielding, part 2 — the ball never reached the hands (gap 65 → 5.7 cm)

The solvers were continuous but the hands could not actually get to a ball on the grass. The in-match gate (`C26_SUITE_PICKUP`) measured the palms **65.4 cm** above the ball. Fixed in three independent layers; final gap **5.7–6.3 cm** against a 14 cm contract.

### Layer 1 — the reach clamp halved the arm (`17f34c8`, pre-existing)

`ReachableArm` in `C26Athlete.cpp` clamped the wrist target to `ArmSpan*0.492f`. **`ArmSpan` is the shoulder→wrist chain (51.8 cm on this rig), not a fingertip-to-fingertip span** — every other limit in the file reads it as `ArmSpan*.95–.98`. The halving capped the hand at half an arm and was the lone outlier. Now `*0.98f`. Effect: 65.4 → 31.4 cm.

### Layer 2 — the pickup's "turf clamp" was not a turf clamp

`SolveFielderPickup` held the hand target at `SafeTake.Z = max(TakeTarget.Z, AnkleZ + 4.f)`.

**`AnkleZ` (11.8) is the height of the ankle JOINT above the rig origin — it is not the ground.** So the floor sat at 15.8 cm in mesh space, level with the fielder's own shins, while the ball the gameplay rests on the grass is at **local Z = −1.40** (measured; = world 3.60 = `BallRadius`, and the rig origin sits 5 cm above the turf). The palms could never get near it, so the match snapped the ball up into the gloves.

The ball is the only ground reference the solver needs — the gameplay never rests it below the turf, so aiming at the ball's own centre cannot put a hand in the ground. Clamp deleted (both phases). The arm-length guard in `AC26Athlete::Animate` is the one that still earns its place.

**The test shared the same misconception and hid it.** `C26Automation.cpp` used `BallGroundTarget = Rig(32,0,16)` (a ball at shin height) and asserted `RightHand.Z >= AnkleZ + 3` — so every assertion passed while the match measured 65 cm. Both corrected: the target is now `TurfZ + BallDiameter*.5` with `TurfZ = -5`, and the new assertion is the one that was missing — the palms' midpoint must *arrive at the ball* (< 5 cm). It fails by ~17 cm against the old floor.

### Layer 3 — the arm was clamped against a shoulder that then moved

`ReachableArm` ran **before** `ShoulderReach`, which protracts the clavicle and carries the socket toward the ball. So the arm was measured against a socket it no longer had, and the elbow stayed bent. Two traps here:

- Re-clamping the **already-clamped** point is a **no-op** — a clamp only ever pulls a target inward, and after protraction that point is *inside* the new reach (44.2 < 50.8). It changed nothing (byte-identical `dCl=44.2`, which is how the no-op was caught).
- The fix is to **re-project the RAW target** from the new socket: `ReachableArm(LHRaw, …)`. Effect: 16.7 → **6.1 cm**.

### The hands must lead the body

The match hands possession over at `ThrowClock = 0.20` (`Simulation.Ball.Position = ReceivingPosition()` from there), and the displayed pose is **filtered** toward the solved one (`PoseLag`, 0.038 s for `Pickup`), so hands that only arrive at 0.20 are still ~16 cm high on screen at the handoff. `SolveFielderPickup` phase 1 now drives the hand lerp with `HandG = SmoothStep(0, 0.17, ActionTime)` while the body keeps `SmoothG` over 0.20. Hand pop is dominated by the throw, so this cost only 7.07 → 7.19 cm against the 8 cm bound.
