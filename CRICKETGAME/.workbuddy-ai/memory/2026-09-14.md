### The defect that mattered — and the trap behind it

The uncommitted deceleration code advanced the braking stride with
`ApproachGait += Dt*ResidualCadence`. **On the real match path `Dt` is always zero**, so it
never advanced.

The reason is worth remembering: `C26MatchGameMode` drives the whole fielding sequence by
**assigning `ActionTime` and calling `Animate(0)`** — the `EventPose` test at
`C26MatchGameMode.cpp:1745` (`I==ActiveFielder && (ThrowClock>=0||CatchClock>=0)`) forces
the zero. The pose clock is authoritative; `Dt` is a no-op for these actions.

**Consequence / rule:** anything in a `Busy` action that is *integrated per frame* is dead
code. Solve it in **closed form from `ActionTime`** instead. That also makes it frame-rate
independent, which per-frame accumulation is not.

### What the fix is

- `SetAction()` captures `ApproachSpeed = max(ShownSpeed, MoveSpeed)` and
  `ApproachGait = GaitPhase` **only on the frame the action changes to `Pickup`** — the
  match squares the fielder up and zeroes `MoveSpeed` immediately afterwards, and the next
  `Animate(0)` snaps `ShownSpeed` to zero too. `ShownSpeed`/`GaitPhase` at that instant are
  exactly what the previous frame's `Running` pose used, so the hand-off is continuous.
- New pure solver `C26Motion::SolveApproachBrake(ActionTime, ApproachSpeed, ApproachGait,
  AnkleZ, ClipLength)` → `FApproachBrake{LeftFoot, RightFoot, PitchL, PitchR, Plant,
  ResidualSpeed, LeanForward, Crouch}`. Speed decays as `exp(-11t)`, gait integrated in
  closed form, gather cross-fades in over the first 0.16 s.
- `FApproachBrake::Plant` **defaults to 1** and the `ApproachSpeed <= 40` early-out returns
  the default. This is load-bearing: with `Plant == 0` the blend `Lerp(Brake.LeftFoot, FL, 0)`
  would teleport both feet to `(0,0,0)` — a far worse bug than the one being fixed.

### Testing trap I walked into — bounds must be speed-scaled

My first test asserted `foot travel < 4 cm per 1/120 s` (copied from the standing-solver
bounds) and "decelerates step over step". **Both failed, and both were wrong, not the code:**

- A **support foot at 700 cm/s covers 5.8 cm in 1/120 s** — that is the ground going past,
  not a skate. A flat few-cm bound is a *standing-pose* bound; it fails every real run.
- Each foot's travel depends on where in the cycle it is; a swing leg snaps through faster
  than the body, so "step N+1 shorter than step N" is **not** an invariant.

Correct framing: bound the **planted** foot tightly (at least one foot is always in support
in a stride, so `min(leftTravel, rightTravel)` is a planted foot), scale the pop bound by
the carried speed, and assert the **residual speed** decays monotonically — that is the
quantity that actually decays.

## Round 3 fielding, part 2 — the ball never reached the hands (gap 65 → 5.7 cm)

The solvers were continuous but the hands could not actually get to a ball on the grass. The
in-match gate (`C26_SUITE_PICKUP`) measured the palms **65.4 cm** above the ball. Fixed in three
independent layers; final gap **5.7–6.3 cm** against a 14 cm contract.

### Layer 1 — the reach clamp halved the arm (`17f34c8`, pre-existing)

`ReachableArm` in `C26Athlete.cpp` clamped the wrist target to `ArmSpan*0.492f`. **`ArmSpan` is
the shoulder→wrist chain (51.8 cm on this rig), not a fingertip-to-fingertip span** — every other
limit in the file reads it as `ArmSpan*.95–.98`. The halving capped the hand at half an arm and
was the lone outlier. Now `*0.98f`. Effect: 65.4 → 31.4 cm.

### Layer 2 — the pickup's "turf clamp" was not a turf clamp

`SolveFielderPickup` held the hand target at `SafeTake.Z = max(TakeTarget.Z, AnkleZ + 4.f)`.

**`AnkleZ` (11.8) is the height of the ankle JOINT above the rig origin — it is not the ground.**
So the floor sat at 15.8 cm in mesh space, level with the fielder's own shins, while the ball the
gameplay rests on the grass is at **local Z = −1.40** (measured; = world 3.60 = `BallRadius`, and
the rig origin sits 5 cm above the turf). The palms could never get near it, so the match snapped
the ball up into the gloves.

The ball is the only ground reference the solver needs — the gameplay never rests it below the
turf, so aiming at the ball's own centre cannot put a hand in the ground. Clamp deleted (both
phases). The arm-length guard in `AC26Athlete::Animate` is the one that still earns its place.

**The test shared the same misconception and hid it.** `C26Automation.cpp` used
`BallGroundTarget = Rig(32,0,16)` (a ball at shin height) and asserted
`RightHand.Z >= AnkleZ + 3` — so every assertion passed while the match measured 65 cm. Both
corrected: the target is now `TurfZ + BallDiameter*.5` with `TurfZ = -5`, and the new assertion is
the one that was missing — the palms' midpoint must *arrive at the ball* (< 5 cm). It fails by
~17 cm against the old floor.

### Layer 3 — the arm was clamped against a shoulder that then moved

`ReachableArm` ran **before** `ShoulderReach`, which protracts the clavicle and carries the socket
toward the ball. So the arm was measured against a socket it no longer had, and the elbow stayed
bent. Two traps here:

- Re-clamping the **already-clamped** point is a **no-op** — a clamp only ever pulls a target
  inward, and after protraction that point is *inside* the new reach (44.2 < 50.8). It changed
  nothing (byte-identical `dCl=44.2`, which is how the no-op was caught).
- The fix is to **re-project the RAW target** from the new socket: `ReachableArm(LHRaw, …)`.
  Effect: 16.7 → **6.1 cm**.

### The hands must lead the body

The match hands possession over at `ThrowClock = 0.20` (`Simulation.Ball.Position =
ReceivingPosition()` from there), and the displayed pose is **filtered** toward the solved one
(`PoseLag`, 0.038 s for `Pickup`), so hands that only arrive at 0.20 are still ~16 cm high on
screen at the handoff. `SolveFielderPickup` phase 1 now drives the hand lerp with
`HandG = SmoothStep(0, 0.17, ActionTime)` while the body keeps `SmoothG` over 0.20. Hand pop is
dominated by the throw, so this cost only 7.07 → 7.19 cm against the 8 cm bound.
